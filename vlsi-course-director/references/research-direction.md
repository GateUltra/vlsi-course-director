# Research and direction

## Ground the lesson in evidence

- Locate the assignment, catalog entry, lesson README, transcript, reference code, examples and exercises. Follow repository instructions and inspect the exact target module; do not assume similarly named labs cover the same topic.
- Record original URL, revision or retrieval date, source paths, license and checksums when archiving. Trace claims to material, mark inferred explanations as interpretations, and distinguish a transcript read from a video actually watched.
- Extract a compact course contract: target learner and prerequisites, goals, scope and exclusions, notation, constraints, source-specific conventions, authoritative examples, edge cases, and unresolved ambiguities. Explicit user feedback supersedes older handoff notes.
- Evaluate executable teaching examples with an independent implementation or manually verified invariants. Include a standard example, at least one instructive counterexample and the meaning of a valid/invalid result. Avoid asserting that an example is optimal without proof.

## Design for comprehension and attention

Ask what the learner can now predict or do. Pick a central question whose cause and effect can be shown, then design a narrative that motivates the problem, introduces only necessary background, exposes a decision or transformation, tests a prediction, and returns to the learning objective. For first-time university learners, explain necessary terms and order of operations without assuming mastery or diluting the technical content. Adjust the depth if the user specifies another audience.

Create a compact beat sheet with scene/cue IDs, visual actions, narration, intended learner inference, factual dependencies and approximate timing. Choose the use of diagrams, kinetic typography, cinematic shots and quiet teaching sequences deliberately; reserve expensive detail for conceptual turning points. Size the film to the lesson and request; 5–7 minutes was a previous project target, not a fixed rule.

When making audiovisual material is authorized and in scope, make an early playable sample that contains at least one complete explanatory transition and representative narration, graphics and captions as requested. An intro montage alone is not enough. Test the selected tools and update the direction on evidence. If the user requests only a plan, do not produce unrequested media. If the user requests a checkpoint or explicitly says to obtain approval, honor it; otherwise work within existing authorization.

Use a course-specific glossary (terms, notation, pronunciation and translations), aligned learning objectives and exercise answers. Maintain source material and creative drafts separately. Preserve attribution and assess reuse permission for each actual source; do not assume that another course shares the precedent's license.
